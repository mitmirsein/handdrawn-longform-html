# Source, quotation, and uncertainty policy

## Preserve source boundaries

The local document is evidence for what its author says. It is not automatically proof that every historical, linguistic, scientific, medical, or theological claim is correct. Keep these distinct in the claim ledger:

```text
TEXT            the author explicitly says it
QUOTE           copied words with source location
VERIFIED        independently checked against an authoritative source
INTERPRETATION  the author’s or speaker’s reading
INFERENCE       the deck’s synthesis from multiple blocks
APPLICATION     recommended response or practice
NEEDS-REVIEW    unresolved or disputed
```

Never “correct” the author silently. Add a note such as “원문 주장; 별도 검증 필요” and offer a source check when it affects the slide’s conclusion.

## Bible and other primary texts

Keep book/chapter/verse or equivalent citation adjacent to the claim. Preserve the user’s supplied translation when quoting it. If a different translation or original-language claim is introduced, name the translation/source and mark the interpretive step. Do not create a composite quotation by stitching non-contiguous verses without saying so.

## External verification

For a claim that will be printed as a fact:

1. Prefer the original institution, report, academic publication, official translation, or primary text.
2. Record publication/retrieval date and the relevant page, verse, table, or line.
3. Record disagreement or uncertainty rather than selecting a precise number because it looks better.
4. Put a compact source on the slide and the fuller citation in `deck.json`/speaker notes.

## Sensitive comparisons

Do not caricature religions, ethnic groups, political groups, or historical communities. If the source makes a comparison, show it as the author’s framing and use neutral visual language. Flag claims that need theological or historical review.

## Copyright and long quotations

Prefer short quotations on the slide and paraphrase the rest in notes. Keep user-provided source text within the user’s requested transformation; obtain or cite external material rather than inventing it. Do not reproduce a whole article or sermon as slide text.
